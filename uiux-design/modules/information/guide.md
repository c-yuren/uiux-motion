# Information design
First identify the task: compare, rank, track change, show part-to-whole, distribution, correlation, process, hierarchy, status or spatial relation. Define source, units, time horizon, denominator, missing and uncertain values. Choose encoding by task and reading precision: position/length for comparisons, line for time, scatter for relationship, table for exact values, graph/diagram for relationships; area/angle only where approximate part-to-whole is suitable. Direct labels and consistent scale beat decorative complexity.
Compact indicators: consider bars, rings, segmented marks, composite glyphs, small multiples and text. Test 0/100, unavailable, mixed periods, reset, long numbers, color blindness and small sizes; separate different units/horizons clearly. Do not use geometric fill as exact proportion if the shape's area does not map linearly, unless explicitly approximate. Keep accessible values and units.
Dashboard: identify primary decision and group supporting evidence, avoid equally weighted cards; preserve comparison baselines and data accuracy. Chart axes and legends must reflect data honestly, avoid misleading truncation and 3D distortion. Visual storytelling may guide attention but not hide uncertainty. Icons need understandable semantics and accessible text; custom shapes must remain recognizable at target size. Use visual and motion modules for styling and transitions, verify data states independently.
